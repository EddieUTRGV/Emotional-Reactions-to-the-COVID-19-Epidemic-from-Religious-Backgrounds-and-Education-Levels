# Emotional Reactions to the COVID-19 Epidemic from Religious Backgrounds

Do spiritual practitioners have an association to the people who perceived the COVID-19 epidemic as a legitimate issue.

Were these religious people more or less likely to adhere to global regulations like wearing masks or keeping the minimum distance?

The research conducted had collected data for categorical variables, most based on the emotional responses many had during the beginning of the pandemic. All participants were 18 years and older and consented to do this survey. The study was focused on a sample based in 4 European countries, Germany, the Netherlands, Spain, and the UK, in April 2020. About 500 participants were recorded in each country.

My explanatory variables topic is about a person’s spiritual identity. My datasets variable name for it is A3.8 and is categorical.

My response variable is about the levels a person believed COVID-19 was exaggerated. My datasets variable name for it is A5.3 and is categorical.

Null Hypothesis: There is no relationship between the frequency in which people believed COVID-19 was not a real issue and religious orientation.

Alternative Hypothesis: There is there is a relationship between the frequency in which people believed COVID-19 to not be a serious issue and religious orientation.

Since both my variables are categorical, a chi-square test would be the most appropriate to assess the relationship.
